A Marathon Runner

Outside the lab, distance running is the other long-term project I work on. Races give me checkpoints, but what I really enjoy is the quiet rhythm of training: building mileage, fine-tuning pace, and seeing how far consistent effort can take me. Here are a few moments that mean a lot to me as a runner.

2025 Suffolk County Marathon
Fast morning race • Road Run

3:03:34.7 Overall ranking: 5/317, Age group ranking: 1/31

Chasing the Half Marathon
Building towards sub-90 • Half marathon

This half marathon was less about the final time and more about learning how to pace myself: staying patient in the first half, accepting discomfort in the middle, and holding form when the legs start to complain. Every attempt brings me one step closer to a stable sub-90 performance.

Quiet Long Run, Clear Mind
Weekend long run • Marathon training

Long runs are where most of the real work happens. I like starting before the city fully wakes up, letting the route and the rhythm slowly clear my head. By the time I finish, I usually have a few new ideas for research—and a much better mood.

Personality

According to my most recent Jungian Archetype Test, I am a person with ESTJ type (extraversion, sensing, feeling and judging).

ESFJs are typically warm, empathetic, and sociable individuals who place a high value on tradition, loyalty, and cooperation. They are practical, detail-oriented, and seek harmony in their relationships with others. I think these descriptions are quite precise on me.

Hobbies

I have many hobbies: reading, eating, and running.

Reading

My favorite book is The Myth of Sisyphus (Le Mythe de Sisyphe) written by Camus. Camus tells a story that Sisyphus, a figure of Greek mythology who was condemned to repeat forever the same meaningless task of pushing a boulder up a mountain, only to see it roll down again just as it nears the top.

The reason why I love The Myth of Sisyphus is that I believe studying/doing research is just pushing a boulder up a mountain. No matter what the results will be, we should consistently keep our pace to do so.

Stay Perseverant and Stay Optimistic.
